# Lucker Party: game-brand reference study

Research date: September 29, 2026. This records the reference analysis, concept
history and selected launcher identity. It covers Portal, Portal 2, Counter-Strike:
Source, Combat Arms, MapleStory, Warcraft III, Mario Party, and Fall Guys.

Current selection: the finalized Clover Peak emblem, Leafcut vector lettering
and four-world V4 hero are implemented in the launcher. The sections below
retain earlier proposals and explain how that selection developed.

Following the research-led concept round, the user preferred the **Party
Assembly** logo and requested a clearly four-leaf clover instead of its initial
three-leaf form. The corrected concept and original are retained separately in
`artifacts/brand-exploration/research-round-v7/`. Hero selection and production
typography/icon refinement remain open.

The following manual comparison found the upper leaf approximately 271 pixels
wide and the lower one 240 pixels wide in the selected raster. An editable SVG
trace and measured lower-leaf correction are now available under that concept
folder's `vector/` directory. The correction matches leaf widths, softens the
exposed lower cleft and uses real path cutouts for the crown clearance. The
original raster and flat vector baseline are retained for comparison; this is
still a design proposal, and no launcher assets were replaced.

The local reference collection contains 47 unchanged source images: wordmarks,
small app icons, retail/library covers, promotional art, and gameplay. It lives
under `artifacts/brand-exploration/reference-study-2026-09-29/`; open `index.html`
to browse it. Each image has a source, era, and provenance. Steam community
icons are labeled separately from desktop shortcut icons. Archived originals
are distinguished from newer marketing and relaunches.

## Main finding

Lucker Party can have a consistent identity while its minigames use different
art styles. Consistency can live in the emblem, typography, navigation,
playlist presentation, player profiles, scoring, and round transitions.

Our interpretation of these references is that a recognizable mark does not
require every environment to share its materials or palette. Portal's flat
graphics coexist with detailed environments; MapleStory's leaf and mushroom
remain recognizable across changing scenes; Warcraft III's packaging uses
the same title and composition across different faction portraits.

The proposed combination is **a clean, constructed host with lucky celebration,
containing specific, convincing minigame worlds**. The host should convey
confidence and warmth. The worlds should demonstrate the variety of play.

## Portal

**Look at:** the wordmark, small community icon, retail cover, and chamber
screenshots. The letter O becomes a portal; the cover gives a falling-person
pictogram enough space to carry the composition. In the chamber, flat signs
and bright functional accents sit against restrained concrete and paneling.

**Lesson:** a small graphic can describe an action, rather than merely decorate
the title. Our modifier and round-state symbols could use similarly economical
shapes: slow time, reversed movement, risk, elimination, victory.

**For our brand:** keep the clover crown readable as a flat silhouette. Soft
concrete and gentle depth belong in host illustrations, not inside every
small icon. Portal's cool restraint also needs more warmth for a party game.

Sources: [Valve's Portal store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/400/),
[archived retail packaging](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Portal).

## Portal 2

**Look at:** the official wordmark, co-op laser chamber, and overgrown facility
screenshot. The simple identity survives environments with broken panels,
exposed machinery, vegetation, and much stronger texture. The co-op chamber
shows colored paths and mechanisms organizing a visually busy space.

**Lesson:** contrast works when the organizing language remains clear. A
consistent interface can sit around worlds that are much more expressive.

**For our brand:** use precise spacing, clear pictograms, and repeatable frames
around varied minigame previews. The clean host should feel intentionally
designed; it does not need a distressed industrial treatment. Portal 2's
weathering is evidence that simplicity in branding and richness in worlds
can coexist, not a recommendation to make our launcher gritty.

Sources: [Valve's original media page](https://www.thinkwithportals.com/media_08.php),
[Portal 2 store screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/620/).

## Counter-Strike: Source

**Look at:** the horizontal title with its soldier silhouette, the compact
community icon, the library cover, and the train/industrial-yard screenshots.
The title is flat and economical; the cover and environments carry the
military subject matter and material detail. The landscape screenshots have
angular architecture, muted surfaces, and strong areas of light and shade.

**Lesson:** visual recognition is distributed across silhouette, proportions,
materials, lighting, and spatial layout. An imitation with only beige walls
and crates would miss much of the character.

**For our brand:** a Source-inspired minigame can retain that visual vocabulary
inside a clean Lucker Party preview frame. Keep military typography local to
the game or pack. These references show the base game's identity; they do
not yet document the distinctive designs of community minigame maps.

Source: [Valve's Counter-Strike: Source store assets](https://store.steampowered.com/app/240/).

## Combat Arms

**Look at:** the original Nexon promotional images alongside Classic relaunch
screenshots. The promotional art uses stenciled lettering, soldiers, weapons,
smoke, and dramatic contrast. One image gives a single soldier dominance;
another presents a cast. The gameplay capture is more modest: straightforward
warehouse geometry, visible weapon framing, and simpler surface detail.

**Lesson:** promotional fidelity and gameplay fidelity are different tasks.
An impressive render does not by itself capture the feeling of an older FPS.
Camera framing, environment construction, and interaction need their own
reference work.

**For our brand:** use scene-specific imagery to establish the FPS experience.
The parent brand can remain welcoming rather than adopt a military mood.
Ensemble composition is useful for showing friends participating, but does
not require one permanent shared character model.

Sources: [Classic relaunch store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1263550/Combat_Arms_the_Classic/),
[archived Nexon promotional art](https://tibruplaygames.blogspot.com/2010/09/disponivel-open-beta-combat-arms-brasil.html),
[archived ensemble art](https://trp420.wordpress.com/2010/09/06/combat-arms-traz-eventos-e-equipamentos-ineditos-em-atualizacao/).

## MapleStory

**Look at:** the early login screen, 2003 town capture, official leaf identity,
and small mushroom icon. The leaf accompanies an expressive wordmark; the
mushroom provides another compact recognition cue. The early scenes combine
large-headed sprites, layered scenery, ladders, platforms, and social labels.
The newer screenshots add richer decoration while retaining side-view staging.

**Lesson:** one brand can have several coordinated assets for different roles.
An icon does not have to be a miniature wordmark. Specific familiar objects
are stronger than a generic description such as "cute fantasy world."

**For our brand:** the crown-clover can be the launcher icon while the title
has a separate typographic treatment. For a 2D minigame, preserve the relevant
sprite proportions, stage construction, and camera conventions. The modern
Frieren crossover header is labeled as current marketing, not early-game art.

Sources: [Nexon's identity](https://maplestory.nexon.com/Guide/N23GameInformation/Articles/307),
[early login archive](https://www.inven.co.kr/board/maple/2316/1607),
[2003 game coverage](https://www.gamemeca.com/view.php?gid=120760),
[modern store screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/216150/).

## Warcraft III

**Look at:** the four original Reign of Chaos box portraits, classic website
logo, and gameplay interface. Different faces and colors occupy the same
cover structure. The metallic fantasy title stays recognizable. Gameplay
uses chunky units, pronounced silhouettes, faction colors, and a substantial
stone-framed interface with portraits and ability icons.

**Lesson:** shared composition can unify very different subjects. Packaging
and gameplay also solve different viewing problems: a large face sells a
faction; exaggerated units and icons support reading a distant battlefield.

**For our brand:** give playlist packs and minigame cards a common title area,
metadata structure, and score treatment while leaving their art distinct.
Top-down game assets need camera-specific readability. Gold bevels and ornate
stone borders can belong to an appropriate minigame without becoming the
Lucker Party interface. These are original-game references, not Reforged.

Sources: [Blizzard's classic site](https://classic.battle.net/war3/),
[archived cover variants](https://lintian.eu/2024/03/01/warcraft-retrospective-17/),
[classic gameplay archive](https://cynicalgamingblog.wordpress.com/2015/11/02/warcraft-3-reign-of-chaosfrozen-throne-campaign-review/).

## Mario Party

**Look at:** the original Mario Party 1/2 covers and Superstars wordmark,
hero composition, and minigame screenshot. The covers make group activity
explicit through overlapping characters, gestures, dice, coins, and stars.
Superstars' hero places a large character and die beside a collage of actual
minigame scenes. Its title has a strong outline and clear color hierarchy.

**Lesson:** party energy can come from composition and visible activity.
Recurring objects work especially well when they correspond to game systems.
A collage can communicate variety more directly than a single generic arena.

**For our brand:** show risk, rounds, and victory through the clover, playlist,
and crown. Give key art a few specific minigame moments in different camera
styles. We need our own typography and staging; Nintendo's cast supplies
recognition that our new brand has not yet earned.

Sources: [Nintendo's original-cover retrospective](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/nintendo-switch-online-expansion-pack-mario-party-and-mario-party-2-are-now-available/),
[Nintendo's Superstars assets](https://www.nintendo.com/jp/switch/az82a/index.html).

## Fall Guys

**Look at:** the official logo, library cover, small community icon, and
obstacle-course screenshots. The bean face appears inside the lettering;
the same character shape dominates the cover and gameplay. Cyan, pink,
bright accents, broad forms, and toy-like obstacles reinforce each other.

**Lesson:** recognition grows when typography, character, palette, and
environment repeatedly support the same idea. This is an unusually unified
world, however, whereas our anthology needs to accommodate different worlds.

**For our brand:** repeat a few distinctive features across our own assets,
such as the crown-clover silhouette and a consistent card treatment. Avoid
combining bean characters, cyan/pink dominance, inflated lettering, and
candy obstacle scenery. Our earlier capsule artwork already came too close
to that combination. A different palette alone would not fix the resemblance.

Sources: [official Fall Guys identity](https://www.fallguys.com/),
[Steam-era art and screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1097150/).

## Proposed brand system

| Layer | Keep consistent | Allow to vary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host | Clover-crown emblem, typography, navigation, palette, layout rhythm | Promotional arrangements and seasonal accents |
| Session | Profiles, player labels/colors, playlist cards, score summaries, modifier symbols, transitions | Preview artwork and game-specific instructional diagrams |
| Minigame | Clear access to session information and results | Camera, characters, rendering, materials, animation, local HUD and effects |

The session layer need not place a permanent host-themed frame on every game
screen. A shared scoreboard on demand and consistent transitions may provide
enough continuity. Player color must be accompanied by a name, symbol, or
other cue; it should not be the only way to recognize a player.

### Emblem and wordmark

Keep exploring the lucky clover crown. Its meaning fits the session: luck
changes the contest, and a winner is crowned. It needs a silhouette we can
recognize without text or color. A stock clover stamped onto a stock crown
could read as a generic luck/casino emblem.

Two promising structural experiments are a crown whose central opening forms
the clover, and a clover whose upper lobes participate in the crown's shape.
Neither is an approved design. Compare them at 16, 24, 32, 64, and 256 pixels
in monochrome before adding material or shadow. Favor broad features and
generous internal spacing over small separate pieces.

Use a bold, constructed wordmark with a small amount of deliberate playfulness.
The recent concept boards lean heavily on bouncy letter placement. More stable
letter proportions, open counters, and one memorable custom feature would
better express our combination of engineering and chaos. The icon, wordmark,
and hero illustration can share proportions without all being rendered as 3D.

### Color and material

Continue evaluating the Lucky Celebration palette:

| Role | Color |
| --- | --- |
| Crown / victory | Butter yellow `#FFD166` |
| Clover / luck | Deep green `#278568` |
| Host background | Cream `#FFF8EC` |
| Celebration / emphasis | Coral `#FF6B76` |
| Typography / boundaries | Navy `#20243B` |

Use navy for dependable lettering and boundaries; yellow is weak against cream
without separation. Do not assume every palette pairing works for small text
or controls. Keep player/team colors separate from the brand accents.

Host illustrations can use softened primitive forms, pale concrete, broad
lighting, and restrained satin surfaces. Keep the primary emblem flat and
clean. Gloss, texture, tiny reflections, and intricate geometry should not
carry recognition at launcher-icon size.

### Key art and minigame previews

An appropriate first hero study would show three very specific experiences:

- A first-person ramp or obstacle challenge with Source-like scale and materials.
- A top-down spell/avoidance arena with readable units and effects.
- A side-view ladder/platform challenge with sprite-based staging.

These are proposed compositions, not confirmed minigames. They should use
different visual languages inside a common playlist or window structure.
Their camera angles can remain different. A central crown and shared profile
markers can connect the session without forcing one avatar across all worlds.

This would communicate the product more honestly than three generic biome
orbs, or a crowd of identical rounded characters. We should choose exact
minigame moments before drawing the next hero illustration.

## What this study does not establish

Images support conclusions about visual form and composition. They do not
verify movement feel, networking, sound, pacing, or the authenticity of a
reimplementation. Source and Warcraft custom-map references remain the main
gap: the base games' logos and screenshots cannot represent every custom map.

Next useful inputs are two or three specific CSS minigame maps and Warcraft
custom-map rounds the team remembers most strongly. Capture their actual
play view, instructions, starting area, and win moment. Use those to develop
our own pack art and instructions after the core mark has been narrowed down.

## Next design comparisons

1. Two or three flat crown-clover constructions, displayed individually.
2. A separate wordmark comparison with restrained versus more playful lettering.
3. The same identity on a launcher tile, profile/score panel, and minigame card.
4. One hero composition using selected gameplay moments and their different styles.

Judge readability, distinctiveness, warmth, and whether the assets explain
the game. Keep these comparisons separate so a good hero rendering cannot
hide a weak small icon.

### Local grip and hand corrections

User review found that rod counts alone missed bad hand-to-rod continuity:
the red-shirt and blue-cap fishers appeared to share a pole. It also found an
extra digit on the warrior's free hand and a malformed mage free hand.
`hero-layered-v2/` contains imagegen corrections applied only through small
local patch masks, then reassembled into the existing four-layer composition.
The two fishing rods now have separate handles and tips; each MapleStory open
hand has four fingers and a thumb. The scene pixels outside the local masks
are verified unchanged, and the CSS/Warcraft native layers are unchanged.
V1 is retained for comparison. Further review must trace prop ownership and
inspect hand silhouettes, rather than merely count objects.

### Wall and cast-shadow seam repairs

User review found a compositing boundary cutting through the right sandstone
wall (different brick courses/tint) and through the concrete platform's face
and ground shadow. V3 adds two local imagegen retouch layers under
`hero-seam-repairs-v3/`, with feathered masks and preserved foreground greenery.
Visual comparison checks continuous mortar courses and natural shadow falloff;
the assembly verifies all pixels outside the patch masks are unchanged. The
original five layers and two optional repair layers are retained in OpenRaster.
This approach avoids another full-canvas redraw and preserves V2's character
and hand corrections. The launcher artwork has not yet been replaced.

### Launcher branding integration

The finalized flat emblem and Leafcut lettering remain unchanged. Their simple
shapes provide a stable identity alongside the more detailed game scenes; adding
hero textures or an outline would weaken the small-size silhouette. The main
home logo is enlarged at the upper-left, with horizontal lockups in Settings
and the multi-instance layout. `BrandLogo` renders the embedded SVG paths
directly, without a raster logo or an additional SVG runtime dependency.

The arena fills the available stage and uses high-quality downsampling. A sand
action strip places the large Play split button at the bottom right, with a
thin darker coral edge, subtle top highlight and soft coral shadow. Hover/press
change elevation; disabled states lose the shadow. Narrow windows reflow the
actions, while the existing cards, contextual version, Settings selection and
update/session guards are retained. This shipped in
[Beta 27](https://github.com/ConcosHut/LuckerParty/releases/tag/v0.3.0-beta.27)
from commit `3fea2ca`, after both platforms passed the complete CI release gate.
